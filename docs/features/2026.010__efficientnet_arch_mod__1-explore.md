# Exploration: EfficientNet Architecture Modifications for Spectral Normalization

## Context

Plan 2026.009 added `enable_spectral_norm` to `dlk/nets/efficientnet1d.py`. Its addendum states the gap: every convolution feeds a `BatchNorm1d`, which removes the scalar $1/\sigma$ again, so the flag bounds only the SE projections and the classifier. This document collects existing approaches and new ideas to close the gap, and does not decide yet what to implement.

**Scope set by the initial questions.**

- **Goal: soft control plus penalties.** Spectral normalization should meaningfully constrain each layer. A certified global Lipschitz bound is not required; the penalties of plan 2026.008 (gradient, finite-difference, spectral norm regularizer) do the rest.
- **Uses:** WGAN critic, a stem or encoder composed in `dlk/nets/compose.py` (in practice also a critic), and a plain classifier.
- **Compatibility: opt-in switches.** With all new options at their defaults, the network stays bit-identical to today's faithful EfficientNet.
- **Components in scope:** the three `BatchNorm1d` layers of `MBConv1D`, the two of `FusedMBConv1D`, the stem and head normalizations, the SE gate, residual scaling, the operator norm of convolutions with $k > 1$, and the activations. Batch-independent normalizations (`LayerNorm`, `GroupNorm`) together with gradient penalties are explicitly in scope.

## Nomenclature

- **Lipschitz constant** $L(f)$: the smallest $L$ with $\|f(x) - f(y)\| \le L \|x - y\|$. For a composition, $L(f \circ g) \le L(f) L(g)$.
- **Spectral normalization (SN)**: reparametrize $W \mapsto W / \sigma(W)$, with $\sigma$ the largest singular value, estimated by power iteration ([Miyato et al., 2018]).
- **Scale invariance**: a layer $N$ is scale invariant if $N(c z) = N(z)$ for every $c > 0$. Every normalization that divides by a statistic of its own input is scale invariant, up to `eps`.
- **Positively homogeneous**: $g(c z) = c\, g(z)$ for $c > 0$. Linear layers without bias, ReLU, and average pooling are; SiLU, sigmoid, and any layer with a bias are not.
- **Batch coupling**: the output for sample $i$ depends on the other samples in the batch. `BatchNorm1d` in train mode couples the batch; `LayerNorm`, `GroupNorm`, and `InstanceNorm` do not.
- **Pre-norm / post-norm**: a residual block $x + f(N(x))$ normalizes the branch input (pre-norm). A block $x + N(f(x))$, or one with $N$ inside $f$ after a convolution, normalizes the branch output (post-norm). EfficientNet is post-norm.
- **Positions** of the normalizations in a block: in `MBConv1D`, **P1** after the expansion convolution, **P2** after the depthwise convolution, **P3** after the projection convolution. In `FusedMBConv1D`, **F1** after the fused convolution and **F2** after the projection. **S** is the stem and **H** the head.
- **Reshaped kernel matrix**: PyTorch normalizes a `Conv1d` weight of shape `(out, in/groups, k)` as an `(out, in/groups · k)` matrix. For $k > 1$, the operator norm of the convolution can exceed $\sigma$ of this matrix by up to $\sqrt{k}$ ([Sedghi et al., 2019]).
- **SE gate**: $x \mapsto x \cdot \mathrm{sigmoid}(f(\mathrm{mean}_t(x)))$, as in `SqueezeExcitation1DLinear`.
- **NF network**: a normalizer-free network that replaces normalization layers by careful initialization and residual scaling ([Brock et al., 2021a]; [Brock et al., 2021b]).

## 1. Diagnosis

### 1.1 Scale invariance cancels more than the preceding convolution

Let $N$ be scale invariant and let $g$ be the chain of layers between the last non-homogeneous layer and $N$. If every layer in $g$ is positively homogeneous, then $N(g(W x / \sigma)) = N(g(W x))$, so $N$ removes the $1/\sigma$ of **every** convolution in that chain, not only the one directly before it.

- With ReLU, a normalization cancels all SN back to the previous normalization.
- With SiLU, the cancellation is exact only for the convolution directly before $N$, and partial further upstream: SiLU is close to linear for small inputs and close to ReLU for large ones.
- A bias between the convolution and $N$ breaks exact invariance. EfficientNet uses `bias=False` everywhere, so the invariance is exact. A bias does not rescue SN, though: $N(W x / \sigma + b) = N(W x + \sigma b)$, so SN only rescales the bias, which training absorbs (Section 3.7).

**Consequence.** Replacing `BatchNorm1d` by `LayerNorm`, `GroupNorm`, `InstanceNorm`, filter response normalization ([Singh and Krishnan, 2020]), or weight standardization ([Qiao et al., 2019]; Scaled WS of [Brock et al., 2021a]) does **not** make SN bind. All of them are scale invariant: weight standardization of $W/\sigma$ equals that of $W$. These replacements fix a different problem, batch coupling (Section 1.2).

The same argument applies to the `SpectralNormRegularizer` of plan 2026.008, Part C: penalizing $\sigma$ of a convolution that feeds a scale-invariant layer shrinks the weight without changing the function. In effect it acts as weight decay, which raises the effective learning rate of that layer.

### 1.2 Two independent axes

Every normalization choice sits on two axes:

|                   | Scale invariant (SN cancelled)  | Not scale invariant (SN binds)                                                                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Batch coupled** | `BatchNorm1d` (train mode)      | mean-only BN ([Salimans and Kingma, 2016]), train mode                                                 |
| **Per sample**    | LN, GN, IN, FRN, WS, EvoNorm-S0 | CenterNorm ([Qi et al., 2023]), affine-only, no normalization, floored normalization (Section 3.1, N5) |

**A critic needs the per-sample row.** [Gulrajani et al., 2017] state that batch normalization turns the critic into a map from a batch to a batch, so the per-sample gradient penalty is no longer valid. They recommend layer normalization instead. The finite-difference penalties of plan 2026.008 have the same problem: the pairs are evaluated in batches whose statistics differ from the training batch.

**SN needs the right-hand column.** Only the lower-right cell serves both needs.

### 1.3 A per-sample scale-invariant normalization blinds the critic to amplitude

The stem convolution has `bias=False`. If a per-sample scale-invariant normalization (LN, GN, IN) follows it, then $D(c x) = D(x)$ for every $c > 0$, up to `eps`: the critic cannot see the overall amplitude of an input time series. `BatchNorm1d` does not have this problem, because its statistics come from the batch or the running estimates. `InstanceNorm`, or GN with one group per channel, removes even the per-channel amplitude and offset over time.

Ways out: a bias in the stem, a non-scale-invariant normalization at S, or no normalization at S.

### 1.4 Where the current bound breaks

| Component                                | Lipschitz with SN on, today                                                   | Cause                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 1x1 convolutions (expand, project, head) | $\le 1$ per layer, cancelled by the following BN                              | P1, P3, H                                   |
| Depthwise and fused convolutions, stem   | $\le \sqrt{k}$ per layer, cancelled by the following BN                       | reshaped kernel matrix; P2, F1, S           |
| `BatchNorm1d`                            | unbounded: $\lvert\gamma\rvert / \sqrt{\mathrm{var} + \epsilon}$ in eval mode | scale invariance                            |
| SE gate                                  | unbounded in $\lvert x \rvert$                                                | derivative $g + x\,\nabla g$ grows with $x$ |
| Residual sum                             | $1 + L_\text{branch}$ per block, a product over blocks                        | no residual scaling                         |
| SiLU                                     | $\approx 1.10$                                                                | —                                           |
| Element-wise dropout (train mode)        | $1/(1-p)$                                                                     | `dropout_connect`, `dropout_head`           |

## 2. Survey of Existing Approaches

### 2.1 GAN critics in practice

- **WGAN-GP** ([Gulrajani et al., 2017]): no BN in the critic; layer normalization as the replacement. The penalty does the Lipschitz control.
- **SNGAN** ([Miyato et al., 2018]): SN on every layer, no normalization in the critic. The ResNet critic uses plain residual sums.
- **BigGAN** ([Brock et al., 2019]): SN in both G and D; BN only in G (class-conditional), none in D.
- **StyleGAN2** ([Karras et al., 2020]): no normalization in D; residual blocks scaled by $1/\sqrt{2}$; equalized learning rate; R1 penalty. The minibatch standard deviation layer couples the batch deliberately.
- **Large-scale study** ([Kurach et al., 2019]): compares BN, LN, and SN in the discriminator, together with gradient penalties. It recommends SN as the default normalization and finds GP helpful in addition.
- **Projected GANs** ([Sauer et al., 2021]): use a frozen, pretrained **EfficientNet-Lite** as the discriminator's feature network, with small SN heads on top. EfficientNet-Lite drops SE and replaces Swish with ReLU6. The frozen backbone runs BN in eval mode, so it acts as a fixed affine map, and the trainable part is small and SN-bounded.
- **SN and signal decay** ([Lin et al., 2021]): SN with $\sigma = 1$ per layer makes activations shrink with depth when there is no normalization. Bidirectional scaled SN rescales by a constant to counter this.

### 2.2 Batch-independent normalizations evaluated on EfficientNet

- **EvoNorm** ([Liu et al., 2020]): fused normalization-activation layers, found by search and evaluated on EfficientNets. The S series is per sample; S0 divides by a group standard deviation, so it is scale invariant up to its sigmoid gate.
- **Proxy normalization** ([Labatie et al., 2021]) and its application to EfficientNet ([Masters et al., 2021]): GN or LN plus a proxy correction recovers BN accuracy without batch dependence.
- **ConvNeXt** ([Liu et al., 2022]): an inverted bottleneck like MBConv, with **one** LN per block instead of three BNs ("fewer normalization layers"), placed after the depthwise convolution, and a learnable per-channel residual scale (LayerScale).

### 2.3 Normalizer-free residual networks

- **Fixup** ([Zhang et al., 2019]), **SkipInit** ([De and Smith, 2020]), **ReZero** ([Bachlechner et al., 2021]): residual branch $x + \alpha f(x)$ with $\alpha$ initialized at 0 (or the last layer zero-initialized). Deep networks train without normalization.
- **NF-ResNets and NFNets** ([Brock et al., 2021a]; [Brock et al., 2021b]): $x_{l+1} = x_l + \alpha f_l(x_l / \beta_l)$ with $\beta_l$ the expected standard deviation of $x_l$; Scaled WS on every convolution; adaptive gradient clipping. Scaled WS is scale invariant (Section 1.1), so it would cancel SN.

### 2.4 Lipschitz-aware normalizations

- **CenterNorm** ([Qi et al., 2023], LipsFormer): $y = \gamma \odot \frac{D}{D-1}(x - \mathrm{mean}(x)) + \beta$, without the division by the standard deviation. Lipschitz with constant $\frac{D}{D-1} \max\lvert\gamma\rvert$. The paper argues LN is not Lipschitz because of the $1/\sigma(x)$ factor, and pairs CenterNorm with spectral initialization and a weighted residual shortcut. Spectral initialization divides each Xavier-initialized weight once by its $\sigma$, so every layer starts 1-Lipschitz but is not constrained afterwards; the weighted residual shortcut is $x + \alpha \odot f(x)$ with a learnable per-channel $\alpha$ initialized small (0.1 to 0.2), which lowers the block's Lipschitz constant from $1 + L_f$ to $1 + \max(\alpha) L_f$.
- **Mean-only BN with weight normalization** ([Salimans and Kingma, 2016]): subtract the batch mean only; the scale comes from the weights.
- **Constrained BN** ([Gouk et al., 2021]): treat eval-mode BN as a diagonal affine map and clip $\lvert\gamma_c\rvert / \sqrt{\mathrm{var}_c + \epsilon}$ to the target constant.
- **BN and adversarial vulnerability** ([Galloway et al., 2019]): BN increases the sensitivity to input perturbations, which agrees with the unbounded gain in Section 1.4.

### 2.5 Lipschitz-by-construction layers

- **GroupSort / MaxMin** ([Anil et al., 2019]): gradient-norm-preserving activations. With ReLU-like activations and SN, a critic cannot reach gradient norm 1 everywhere, so it underestimates the Wasserstein distance; penalties partly compensate.
- **Invertible ResNets** ([Behrmann et al., 2019]): SN with coefficient $c < 1$ on the residual branch.
- **AOL** ([Prach and Lampert, 2022]): rescale $W$ by a diagonal matrix computed from $\lvert W^\top W\rvert$; certified 1-Lipschitz without iteration, for dense and convolutional layers.
- **SLL** ([Araujo et al., 2023]): residual layers $x - 2 W T^{-1} \sigma(W^\top x + b)$, 1-Lipschitz by construction, and a unifying SDP view of SN, AOL, orthogonal layers, and convex potential layers.
- **LiResNet** ([Hu et al., 2023]): a 1-Lipschitz residual network without BN, scaled to ImageNet.
- **Benchmark** ([Prach et al., 2024]): compares 1-Lipschitz layers by memory, speed, and certified accuracy.

These layers give certified bounds, which is more than the scope asks for. They serve as references for single components, not as a replacement of the block.

### 2.6 Operator norm of convolutions

- **Exact norm by power iteration on the operator** ([Farnia et al., 2019]): alternate `conv1d` and `conv_transpose1d` instead of multiplying with the reshaped matrix.
- **Circulant / Fourier view** ([Sedghi et al., 2019]): for circular padding, the operator norm is $\max_\omega \sigma_\text{max}(\hat W(\omega))$ over the per-frequency $C_\text{out} \times C_\text{in}$ matrices.
- **Gram iteration** ([Delattre et al., 2023]) and **zero padding** ([Delattre et al., 2024]): fast, differentiable upper bounds, also for zero padding.

### 2.7 SE alternatives

- **No SE**: EfficientNet-Lite, and the Fused-MBConv stages of EfficientNetV2.
- **ECA** ([Wang et al., 2020]): a 1D convolution across the pooled channel vector instead of the bottleneck MLP; still a multiplicative gate.

## 3. Idea Catalog by Component

Each idea has an identifier, its effect with SN on, and its cost or risk. Ideas marked *new* are not taken from a cited source.

### 3.1 Normalization (positions P1–P3, F1–F2, S, H)

| ID  | Idea                                                                                         | Batch independent      | SN binds                                | Notes                                                                 |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| N0  | keep `BatchNorm1d` (today)                                                                   | no                     | no                                      | invalid for a per-sample penalty                                      |
| N1  | remove the normalization at chosen positions                                                 | yes                    | yes, upstream to the next normalization | loses stabilization; Section 3.2 combines with residual scaling       |
| N2  | LN or GN (e.g., `ChannelLayerNorm` or `Normalization` from `conv1d.py`)                      | yes                    | no                                      | fixes batch coupling only; amplitude blindness at S (Section 1.3)     |
| N3  | CenterNorm (per sample, over channels)                                                       | yes                    | yes                                     | Lipschitz $\approx \max\lvert\gamma\rvert$; no variance control       |
| N4  | affine-only (per-channel $\gamma$, $\beta$), $\lvert\gamma\rvert$ clamped or SN-like bounded | yes                    | yes                                     | equivalent to frozen BN; the Projected GAN backbone is a special case |
| N5  | *new:* floored normalization $y = \gamma\,(x - \mu) / \sqrt{s^2 + \tau^2}$, per sample       | yes                    | partially                               | see below                                                             |
| N6  | eval-mode BN in the critic, gain clamped per [Gouk et al., 2021]                             | yes (fixed statistics) | yes                                     | running statistics drift; train and eval differ                       |
| N7  | mean-only BN                                                                                 | no                     | yes                                     | batch coupled; only for the classifier use                            |

**N5 in detail.** Let $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be the entries normalized together (for LN over a 1D feature map, $n = C \cdot T$). The centering projection $P = I - \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ subtracts the mean, so $c = P x = x - \mu$ is the centered input; $P$ is symmetric, $P^2 = P$, and $\lVert P \rVert = 1$. With $s^2 = \lVert c \rVert^2 / n$ and $r = \sqrt{s^2 + \tau^2}$, the chain rule gives the Jacobian of $c / r$ with respect to $x$ as $\frac{1}{r}\big(I - \frac{c c^\top}{n r^2}\big) P$: the bracket is the Jacobian with respect to $c$, and $P$ is the Jacobian of $c$ with respect to $x$. The matrix $\frac{1}{r}\big(I - \frac{c c^\top}{n r^2}\big)$ has eigenvalues $1/r$ orthogonal to $c$ and $\tau^2 / r^3$ along $c$, both at most $1/\tau$, and $\lVert P \rVert = 1$ does not increase the norm. Hence $L \le \max\lvert\gamma\rvert / \tau$. For $s \gg \tau$ it behaves like LN; for $s \ll \tau$ it is a centering followed by the fixed gain $1/\tau$, where SN binds. `eps` in today's normalizations is this $\tau^2$, so N5 is LN or GN with a large `eps`, chosen relative to the activation scale. Open question: how to set $\tau$ when the activation scale changes during training. One option is a fixed $\tau$ together with SN, which keeps activations at a predictable scale.

**Where to keep one normalization.** By Section 1.1, the only position that cancels no SN inside the branch is the branch input on the residual stream, i.e., pre-norm (N8 below). ConvNeXt's position after the depthwise convolution cancels the depthwise SN and, with SiLU, partly the expansion SN.

| ID  | Block layout with one normalization             | Cancels SN of                                      |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| N8  | *pre-norm:* $x + \alpha f(N(x))$, no P1, P2, P3 | nothing in the branch                              |
| N9  | ConvNeXt-like: P2 only                          | depthwise, partly expand                           |
| N10 | P1 only (normalize the expanded tensor)         | expand                                             |
| N11 | P3 only (today's last BN)                       | project, and partly everything back to the SE gate |

Pre-norm also makes the branch input independent of the growing scale of the residual stream, which counters the SN signal decay of [Lin et al., 2021]. Blocks without a residual (stride 2 or a channel change) need their own normalization at the input, or none.

### 3.2 Residual connection

| ID  | Form                                                                      | Block Lipschitz                    | Notes                                                                 |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| R0  | $x + f(x)$ (today)                                                        | $1 + L_f$                          | grows with depth as a product                                         |
| R1  | $x + \alpha f(x)$, learnable $\alpha$ initialized at 0 (SkipInit, ReZero) | $1 + \lvert\alpha\rvert L_f$       | needs a bound on $\alpha$ (clamp or $\tanh$) to stay bounded          |
| R2  | per-channel LayerScale                                                    | $1 + \max\lvert\alpha_c\rvert L_f$ | as in ConvNeXt                                                        |
| R3  | $(x + f(x)) / \sqrt{2}$ (StyleGAN2)                                       | $(1 + L_f)/\sqrt{2}$               | fixed, no parameters                                                  |
| R4  | convex $(1 - \alpha) x + \alpha f(x)$, $\alpha \in [0, 1]$                | $\le \max(1, L_f)$                 | with $L_f \le 1$ the whole stack is $\le 1$                           |
| R5  | NF-style $x + \alpha f(x / \beta)$                                        | $1 + \alpha L_f / \beta$           | $\beta$ from the expected stream scale; designed for no normalization |

**Dropout.** The blocks apply element-wise `nn.Dropout` (`dropout_connect`) before the residual sum, which scales by $1/(1-p)$ in train mode and makes $D$ random within one penalty evaluation. For a critic, set `dropout_connect=0` and `dropout_head=0`, or switch to per-sample drop path. Plan 2026.009, Section 7, already notes that the comment calls this stochastic depth.

### 3.3 SE gate

| ID  | Idea                                                                                            | Effect                                                                                                               |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| S0  | keep (today)                                                                                    | unbounded in $\lvert x \rvert$                                                                                       |
| S1  | remove (EfficientNet-Lite; set `se_ratio=None`, already possible via configs)                   | no new code; loses channel attention                                                                                 |
| S2  | bound the gate input: SE after a layer that bounds $\lvert x \rvert$ (e.g., ReLU6, A2), or clip | the extra Jacobian term scales with $\max \lvert x \rvert \cdot L_f / 4$, so it is bounded once $\lvert x \rvert$ is |
| S3  | *new:* additive excitation $x + h(\mathrm{mean}_t(x))$ (shift instead of scale)                 | $L \le 1 + L_h$ in $\ell_2$ (pooling gives $1/\sqrt{T}$, broadcasting back gives $\sqrt{T}$); keeps global context   |
| S4  | ECA gate                                                                                        | same issue as S0, fewer parameters                                                                                   |
| S5  | gate computed from `x.detach()`                                                                 | anti-pattern: changes the gradient that the penalty sees, not the function                                           |

### 3.4 Convolution operator norm

| ID  | Idea                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Bound                                                           | Cost                                |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| C0  | SN of the reshaped kernel matrix (today)                                                                                                                                                                                          | $\sqrt{k}$                                                      | one power-iteration step            |
| C1  | C0 divided by $\sqrt{k}$                                                                                                                                                                                                          | 1, loose                                                        | none                                |
| C2  | *new for 1D:* per-channel depthwise SN: divide each filter $w_c$ by $\max(1, \lVert \hat w_c \rVert_\infty)$, with $\hat w_c$ the DFT of $w_c$ zero-padded to $n \gg k$ points; cheap certified alternative $\lVert w_c \rVert_1$ | exact up to DFT sampling; per channel, so no over-normalization | one `rfft` of a `(C, n)` tensor     |
| C3  | power iteration on the operator (Farnia et al.) with `conv1d` / `conv_transpose1d`                                                                                                                                                | exact for the given length and padding                          | one extra conv pair per step        |
| C4  | frequency-domain SN for dense convolutions: batched power iteration on the per-frequency matrices $\hat W(\omega)$                                                                                                                | exact for circular padding, upper bound for zero padding        | 1D keeps it cheap                   |
| C5  | AOL rescaling                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | certified 1                                                     | one $W^\top W$ product              |
| C6  | SN with a target $c \ne 1$ ($c W / \sigma$), fixed or bounded learnable                                                                                                                                                           | $c$                                                             | counters signal decay in NF layouts |

C2 is the natural fit for MBConv: the depthwise operator norm is the maximum over channels, and the joint $(C, k)$ normalization of today shrinks every channel by the worst one (plan 2026.009, Section 10). Stride only subsamples and does not increase the norm.

### 3.5 Activations

- **A0** keep SiLU ($\approx 1.10$). Sufficient for soft control, and smooth, which suits a gradient penalty that differentiates through the critic twice.
- **A1** GroupSort / MaxMin: gradient-norm preserving. Works channel-wise after the expansion; not a drop-in after the depthwise convolution, which mixes no channels. Matters mainly for certified bounds.
- **A2** ReLU6 (EfficientNet-Lite): bounded output, which also bounds the SE gate input (S2). Not smooth.

### 3.6 Stem and head

- **Stem:** N1, N3, or N5 at S, or N2 plus a stem bias (Section 1.3). C2 or C3 for the $k = 3$, stride 2 convolution.
- **Head:** the 1x1 convolution to 1280 channels feeds BN and SiLU, so its SN is cancelled. Same normalization choice as in the blocks; or drop H. Average pooling over length $T$ is $1/\sqrt{T}$-Lipschitz in $\ell_2$ from the feature map to the pooled vector. Set `dropout_head=0` in the critic. A minibatch standard deviation feature (StyleGAN2) couples the batch and conflicts with the per-sample penalty.

### 3.7 Convolution biases

Every convolution has `bias=False` today, because a per-channel bias directly before `BatchNorm1d` is redundant: BN subtracts the per-channel mean, bias included, and its $\beta$ adds a learnable shift back. What turning the bias on means depends on what follows the convolution:

| Followed by | Effect of `bias=True` |
| --- | --- |
| BN, or IN / GN with one group per channel | none: the per-channel mean removes it exactly |
| LN over channels, GN with fewer groups | not redundant (a channel pattern survives), but SN is still cancelled (Section 1.1) |
| CenterNorm or affine-only (N3, N4) with $\beta$ | mostly redundant with $\beta$ |
| nothing (N1, configurations C and E) | **needed**: it takes over the role of BN's $\beta$ |

- **Lipschitz constant:** a bias is a translation, so it does not change the Lipschitz constant, and SN does not touch it. Biases are free in this respect.
- **Expressivity without normalization:** without biases and normalization, a network with ReLU is positively homogeneous, $D(c x) = c\, D(x)$, and with SiLU it is close to that for large inputs. Biases break this. Every position whose normalization is removed should therefore get `bias=True` on the convolution before it.
- **Amplitude blindness (Section 1.3):** a bias at S before a per-sample normalization over channels or groups makes the output depend on the input amplitude again, because $N(c W x + b) \ne N(W x + b)$. It does not help before IN, which removes the bias.
- **Bias as a soft floor:** before LN over channels, a bias pattern with variance $v$ across channels adds roughly $v$ to the variance that LN divides by, much like $\tau^2$ in N5. Unlike $\tau$, it is learnable and can shrink to 0, so it gives no guaranteed bound.
- **Mean shift without normalization:** SiLU has a positive mean output, and without normalization the mean of the residual stream grows with depth ([Brock et al., 2021a] motivates Scaled WS with this). Biases can learn to compensate. CenterNorm removes the shift by construction.
- **Cost:** few parameters; the zero-bias initialization in `_initialize_weights` already covers them; with the bias on, checkpoints gain `.bias` keys and do not load across the setting.

## 4. Candidate Configurations

Combinations of the ideas above, ordered from the smallest to the largest deviation from EfficientNet.

| Config                        | Normalization                     | Residual | SE       | Conv  | Batch independent | SN binds in the body     | Deviation | Training risk         |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------- | -------- | ----- | ----------------- | ------------------------ | --------- | --------------------- |
| **A** batch-independent       | N2 at all positions, stem bias    | R0       | S0       | C0    | yes               | no                       | small     | low                   |
| **B** remove some norms       | N2 at P2 only (N9), none at P1/P3 | R1/R2    | S0 or S1 | C0    | yes               | partly (expand, project) | medium    | low–medium            |
| **C** pre-norm                | N8 with N2 or N5                  | R1/R4    | S2/S3    | C2    | yes               | yes                      | medium    | medium                |
| **D** in-place Lipschitz norm | N3 or N5 at all positions         | R0 or R3 | S2       | C2    | yes               | yes (N3), mostly (N5)    | small     | medium                |
| **E** normalizer-free         | N1 everywhere                     | R5 + C6  | S1 or S3 | C2/C3 | yes               | yes                      | large     | high on deep variants |

- **A** is today's architecture made valid for per-sample penalties. SN stays decorative in the body; the penalty does all Lipschitz control.
- **B** is the "remove some of the three BatchNorms" route. It follows ConvNeXt's evidence that one normalization per inverted bottleneck is enough.
- **C** is the cleanest fit for "SN on every layer plus one stabilizing normalization": the normalization sits on the residual stream and cancels no SN in the branch.
- **D** keeps the topology unchanged and swaps the normalization class only. It is the smallest code change that makes SN bind.
- **E** follows SNGAN and NFNets. It is the most likely to need retuning, especially for the V2-S to XL depths.

All configurations combine with the penalties of plan 2026.008. The penalty is valid for all of them, because all are batch independent.

## 5. Switch Design Sketch (opt-in)

To decide later. A sketch in the style of `ConvResNet`, whose blocks already take a `normalization: NormalizationFactory | bool` argument (`dlk/nets/conv1d.py`):

- `normalization`: a factory or `False`, default `nn.BatchNorm1d`, applied at the positions selected by `normalization_positions`, which defaults to all positions (P1, P2, P3, F1, F2, S, H).
- `normalization_placement`: `"post"` (today) or `"pre"` (N8).
- `residual_scale`: `None` (today), `"skipinit"`, `"layerscale"`, `"convex"`, `"sqrt2"`.
- `se_mode`: `"gate"` (today) or `"shift"` (S3); removal stays `se_ratio=None` in the configs.
- `depthwise_spectral_norm`: `"matrix"` (today) or `"per_channel"` (C2), effective only with `enable_spectral_norm=True`.
- `stem_bias`: `False` (today).
- `conv_bias`: `False` (today); or derived automatically, `True` exactly at the positions whose normalization is removed (Section 3.7).

Each option threads through `ScalableEfficientNet1D`, `MBConv1D`, `FusedMBConv1D`, and the 20 named variants, as `enable_spectral_norm` does. A single preset argument (e.g., `lipschitz_preset="critic"`) could bundle a recommended combination, so the named variants need one new argument instead of several.

## 6. Experiments to Decide

Cheap checks on `EfficientNetV2BB0` and `EfficientNetV1BB0` (CPU), then on a larger variant (GPU):

1. **Cancellation check:** for each position, measure the change in output when $\sigma$ of each convolution is scaled by 2. It confirms which SN binds under each configuration.
2. **Signal propagation plots** ([Brock et al., 2021a]) per block, with SN on, for configurations B–E at initialization.
3. **Empirical Lipschitz:** the distribution of $\lVert \nabla_x D \rVert$ on real, generated, and interpolated samples, and the finite-difference quotients of plan 2026.008, during critic training.
4. **Amplitude sensitivity:** $D(c x)$ against $c$ for configurations with a per-sample normalization at S (Section 1.3).
5. **Training outcome:** WGAN training on the FHN data with each configuration plus a penalty: Wasserstein estimate, stability across seeds, sample quality; and classification accuracy for the classifier use.

## 7. Open Questions

1. Which configuration (A–E) should be planned first? A recommendation for a first plan: **D with N5 or N3, plus C2 and S2**, because it keeps the topology and swaps classes only, and **C** as the second step if D trains poorly.
2. How large are the critics in practice: only the minimal variants, or also V2-S and larger? Configuration E becomes riskier with depth.
3. Should the network see the input amplitude (Section 1.3), or is invariance to scale acceptable or even desired for some data?
4. Is a fixed $\tau$ in N5 acceptable, or should it adapt to the scale of the activations?
5. Is per-sample drop path wanted as a replacement for the element-wise `dropout_connect`, or is dropout simply off for critics?

## 8. Unrelated Improvements Observed (report only)

- The element-wise `dropout_connect` and the misleading comment `# Stochastic depth (drop connect)` (plan 2026.009, Section 7) matter more here: a critic should not use it.
- `SpectralNormRegularizer` (plan 2026.008, Part C) acts as weight decay on every convolution that feeds a scale-invariant normalization (Section 1.1). Its usage doc could say so.
