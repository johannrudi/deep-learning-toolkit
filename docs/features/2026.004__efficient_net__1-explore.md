Both models are families of convolutional networks from Google Brain by Tan and Le. EfficientNet (ICML 2019, [V1 paper]) optimizes parameter and FLOP efficiency through compound scaling. EfficientNetV2 (ICML 2021, [V2 paper]) keeps the same design philosophy but re-optimizes for training speed on modern accelerators. The core finding of V2: FLOPs are a poor proxy for wall-clock cost, so V2 trades a few more FLOPs for layers with higher arithmetic intensity, and it changes how the family is scaled and trained.

## 1. EfficientNet (V1, 2019)

### 1.1 Macro-architecture of the baseline B0

The baseline was found by multi-objective neural architecture search (AutoML MNAS) under a FLOP budget. It is a stack of mobile inverted bottleneck blocks (MBConv), borrowed from MobileNetV2/MnasNet, with squeeze-and-excitation (SE) added. Resolution is 224x224 at the input and halves at stages whose first block has stride 2.

| Stage | Operator           | Input res. | Channels | Blocks |
| ----- | ------------------ | ---------- | -------- | ------ |
| 1     | Conv 3x3           | 224x224    | 32       | 1      |
| 2     | MBConv1, k3x3      | 112x112    | 16       | 1      |
| 3     | MBConv6, k3x3      | 112x112    | 24       | 2      |
| 4     | MBConv6, k5x5      | 56x56      | 40       | 2      |
| 5     | MBConv6, k3x3      | 28x28      | 80       | 3      |
| 6     | MBConv6, k5x5      | 14x14      | 112      | 3      |
| 7     | MBConv6, k5x5      | 14x14      | 192      | 4      |
| 8     | MBConv6, k3x3      | 7x7        | 320      | 1      |
| 9     | Conv 1x1, pool, FC | 7x7        | 1280     | 1      |

`MBConv{e}` denotes an inverted bottleneck with expansion ratio $e$. Note the mix of 3x3 and 5x5 kernels and the two stride-1 stages (3 and 7) that stack blocks at constant resolution.

### 1.2 MBConv block anatomy

One MBConv block with expansion ratio $e$, kernel $k$, input channels $C_{in}$, output channels $C_{out}$:

1. Expand: $1 \times 1$ conv $C_{in} \rightarrow e C_{in}$, batch norm, Swish. Skipped when $e = 1$.
2. Spatial filter: $k \times k$ depthwise conv, batch norm, Swish. Stride 1 or 2.
3. Squeeze-excite: global average pool $\rightarrow 1 \times 1$ reduce to $0.25 C_{in} \rightarrow$ Swish $\rightarrow 1 \times 1$ expand back to $e C_{in} \rightarrow$ sigmoid $\rightarrow$ channel-wise rescale.
4. Project: $1 \times 1$ conv $e C_{in} \rightarrow C_{out}$, batch norm. No activation.
5. Skip connection plus stochastic depth (drop connect) when shapes match. Survival probability decays linearly from 1.0 in the first block to 0.8 in the last.

All pointwise convs are dense GEMMs. All spatial filtering rides on depthwise convs, which is the decisive choice for compute intensity (section 4).

### 1.3 Compound scaling

B0 is scaled up by a single coefficient phi applied uniformly to depth d, width w, and input resolution r:

```
d = alpha^phi,  w = beta^phi,  r = gamma^phi
subject to alpha * beta^2 * gamma^2 ≈ 2,  alpha, beta, gamma >= 1
```

A grid search on B0 at phi = 1 gave alpha = 1.2, beta = 1.1, gamma = 1.15. B1 through B7 fix these constants and increase phi, roughly doubling total FLOPs per step. Every stage is scaled by the same factor; dropout grows with model size (0.2 at B0 up to 0.5 at B7).

| B1    | 240   | 7.8M   | 0.70B | 79.2%          |
| B2    | 260   | 9.2M   | 1.0B  | 80.3%          |
| B3    | 300   | 12M    | 1.8B  | 81.7%          |
| B4    | 380   | 19M    | 4.2B  | 83.0%          |

V1's claim was efficiency: B7 matched GPipe accuracy with 8.4x fewer parameters and 6.1x faster CPU inference; B4 beat ResNet-50 by ~6.7 points at similar FLOPs.

### 1.5 The classification head (identical in V2)

The last stage maps the final feature map to class scores. Both families use the same head; only the incoming channel count differs (320 at B0, 256 at V2-S), and both lift it to a 1280-d embedding. It runs in three steps:

1. Conv 1x1: a pointwise convolution lifts the channels to 1280, followed by batch norm and Swish. It is a per-pixel linear projection: at B0 it holds 320x1280 ~ 0.4M weights and costs ~40M FLOPs per image (2x7x7x320x1280). No spatial information is mixed here.
2. Pool: global average pooling reduces the HxW map to a 1280-d vector. This makes the network fully convolutional up to the head: the total stride is 32, so the pooled grid is 7x7 at 224-px input and 12x12 at V2-S's 384-px eval size, and any input size is valid.
3. FC: dropout, then one dense layer 1280 -> 1000 (ImageNet classes) producing the logits. Its 1280x1000 ~ 1.3M weights make it the largest single layer of the head. Softmax turns logits into class probabilities at inference.

Two practical notes. The pooled 1280-d vector is the standard embedding for transfer learning: the published "feature-vector" hub modules stop right after pooling. And the dropout rate is a scaling knob: V1 raises it with model size (0.2 at B0 to 0.5 at B7), while V2 schedules it together with image size during progressive learning (section 2.6).

## 2. EfficientNetV2 (2021)

### 2.1 Three diagnosed bottlenecks of V1

The [V2 paper] starts from a profiling study of V1 on TPU/GPU:

1. Training at very large image sizes is slow. Activation memory grows with r^2, so the large models force small batches. Training B5 at 380 instead of 456 ran up to 2.2x faster with slightly better accuracy (a FixRes-style effect).
2. Depthwise convolutions are slow in early layers. They have few FLOPs but low hardware utilization (section 4). Early stages run at high resolution with few channels, the worst case for depthwise ops.
3. Equal compound scaling of every stage is sub-optimal. Stages contribute unequally to accuracy, speed, and parameter cost, yet V1 multiplies all of them by the same factor.

### 2.2 Fused-MBConv block

Fused-MBConv (first used in EfficientNet-EdgeTPU) merges the expand and depthwise steps of MBConv into one dense convolution:

1. Fused expand+filter: regular $k \times k$ conv $C_{in} \rightarrow e C_{in}$, batch norm, Swish. Skipped as a separate expand when $e = 1$ (the block is then just a dense conv).
2. Project: $1 \times 1$ conv $e C_{in} \rightarrow C_{out}$, batch norm.
3. Skip connection plus stochastic depth when shapes match.

Replacing the depthwise 3x3 plus 1x1 pair with a single dense 3x3 conv raises FLOPs but turns the op into a well-shaped GEMM with high arithmetic intensity. The V2 ablation shows the nuance: swapping Fused-MBConv into the early stages 1-3 of B4 speeds up training with small parameter/FLOP overhead; applying it to all stages inflates parameters and FLOPs and slows training down. Placement matters, so placement was left to NAS. In the resulting models Fused-MBConv carries no SE module; SE appears only in the MBConv stages.

### 2.3 Training-aware NAS

The search space extends V1's: op type {MBConv, Fused-MBConv}, expansion ratio {1, 4, 6}, kernel {3x3, 5x5}, and per-stage layer counts. The space is pruned by reusing V1's channel sizes and dropping skip-pooling options that were never selected. The reward adds measured training step time to accuracy and parameter count: reward = A * S^w * P^v with w = -0.07, v = -0.05. Roughly 1000 candidates were trained for about 10 epochs each at reduced image size.

### 2.4 Macro-architecture of EfficientNetV2-S

| Stage | Operator                  | Stride | Channels | Blocks |
|-------|---------------------------|--------|----------|--------|
| 0     | Conv 3x3                  | 2      | 24       | 1      |
| 1     | Fused-MBConv1, k3x3       | 1      | 24       | 2      |
| 2     | Fused-MBConv4, k3x3       | 2      | 48       | 4      |
| 3     | Fused-MBConv4, k3x3       | 2      | 64       | 4      |
| 4     | MBConv4, k3x3, SE0.25     | 2      | 128      | 6      |
| 5     | MBConv6, k3x3, SE0.25     | 1      | 160      | 9      |
| 6     | MBConv6, k3x3, SE0.25     | 2      | 256      | 15     |
| 7     | Conv 1x1, pool, FC        | -      | 1280     | 1      |

Differences from B0 that the search produced:

- Fused-MBConv in the early high-resolution stages 1-3, MBConv in the late stages 4-6.
- Almost exclusively 3x3 kernels (no 5x5), with more blocks stacked to recover receptive field.
- Smaller expansion ratios (1 and 4) in early blocks; 6 survives only in late stages. Smaller expansion means less memory access per block.
- The last stride-1 stage of V1 (stage 7: 14x14, 192 channels) is removed entirely, cutting parameter count and memory traffic.
- Depth is redistributed toward the late low-resolution stages (15 blocks at 7x7), where extra layers are cheap.

### 2.5 Non-uniform scaling to M, L, XL

V2 keeps compound-style scaling but modifies it:

- More layers are added to later stages only, not uniformly across the network.
- The maximum inference image size is capped at 480 (512 for XL), since very large inputs drive memory and training-time overhead for marginal accuracy.
- V2-B0..B3 are V2-S scaled down with the original V1 compound rule, for like-for-like comparison with V1.

### 2.6 Progressive learning with adaptive regularization

Training runs through phases of increasing image size. Unlike earlier progressive resizing, regularization grows with the image: small images mean low effective capacity, so they pair with weak regularization; large images get strong regularization. The schedule for V2-S:

| Phase | Image | Dropout | RandAugment mag. | Mixup |
|-------|-------|---------|------------------|-------|
| 1     | 128   | 0.1     | 5                | 0     |
| 2     | 160   | 0.1     | 10               | 0     |
| 3     | 300   | 0.2     | 15               | 0.1   |
| 4     | 384   | 0.3     | 20               | 0.2   |

This removes the accuracy drop that plain progressive resizing causes and speeds training further: most epochs run at small resolution with large batches.

## 3. Compute intensity: why V2 is faster per FLOP

### 3.1 Arithmetic intensity of the layer types

Arithmetic intensity (AI) is FLOPs per byte of memory traffic. It decides whether an op is compute-bound or memory-bound on an accelerator.

- Dense kxk conv, C_in = C_out = C, feature map HxW: FLOPs ~ 2*k^2*C^2*H*W against ~2*C*H*W activation elements and k^2*C^2 weights. AI scales with C, so it is high and maps onto tensor cores.
- Depthwise kxk conv: FLOPs ~ 2*k^2*C*H*W against nearly the same activation traffic. AI drops by roughly a factor of C; the op is memory-bandwidth bound. The matrix engines idle while data moves.

Early V1 stages are the pathological case: H and W are large (112x112, 56x56) while C is small (16-40), so depthwise convs there move the most data for the least compute. This is exactly where NAS placed Fused-MBConv in V2. Fusing the expand 1x1 and depthwise 3x3 into one dense 3x3 conv multiplies FLOPs but also multiplies AI, reduces kernel launches and activation round-trips, and uses tensor cores. Net effect: faster in wall-clock despite more nominal work.

### 3.2 Resolution, memory, and batch size

Activation memory scales with r^2 * w. V1's compound rule pushes r to 600 at B7, which shrinks the training batch and underutilizes the accelerator. V2 caps r at 480/512 and adds capacity through depth in cheap late stages instead of through resolution. Progressive learning additionally spends the majority of epochs at 128-300 px, so average step time and memory are far below the eval-time cost.

### 3.3 FLOPs vs wall clock

The families cross over in an instructive way:

- V2-S (83.9%) has about 2x the FLOPs of V1-B4 (8.4B vs 4.2B) at ~1 point higher accuracy, yet trains and infers faster. FLOPs alone would predict the opposite.
- Under identical training settings, V2-M matches V1-B7 accuracy with 17% fewer params, 37% fewer FLOPs, 4.1x faster training, and 3.1x faster inference.
- End to end, V2 trains 5x-11x faster than V1/ViT/NFNet baselines at comparable accuracy and is up to 6.8x smaller than peer models.

The lesson: V1 optimized FLOPs and params; V2 optimized measured step time. Dense fused convs, moderate resolution, and depth in late stages all move compute from memory-bound to compute-bound layers.

## 4. Differences at a glance

| Aspect              | EfficientNet (V1)            | EfficientNetV2                    |
|---------------------|------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Block types         | MBConv only                  | Fused-MBConv early, MBConv late   |
| SE placement        | every block                  | MBConv blocks only                |
| Expansion ratios    | 1 and 6                      | 1, 4, 6 (prefers smaller)         |
| Kernel sizes        | mixed 3x3 and 5x5            | almost all 3x3, deeper stacks     |
| Stride-1 tail stage | present (14x14, 192 ch)      | removed                           |
| Depth distribution  | uniform across stages        | concentrated in late stages       |
| Scaling rule        | compound, uniform (alpha^phi)| non-uniform; later stages get more|
| Max input size      | 600 (B7)                     | 480 (XL: 512)                     |
| NAS objective       | accuracy + FLOPs             | accuracy + params + train step time|
| Training            | fixed large resolution       | progressive size + adaptive reg.  |
| Head                | 1280-ch 1x1 conv + pool + FC | same                              |
| Optimization target | params/FLOPs efficiency      | training speed + params           |

## 5. Takeaways

1. The block inventory is the headline change: V2 adds Fused-MBConv and uses it where depthwise convs are memory-bound (early, high-resolution, few channels).
2. V2 deliberately accepts more FLOPs in exchange for higher arithmetic intensity and better accelerator utilization. FLOPs are a budget metric, not a speed metric.
3. Scaling changed shape: V1 grows everything uniformly to huge resolutions; V2 caps resolution and adds cheap late-stage depth.
4. Progressive learning with size-matched regularization is a training-time, not an architecture, change — and it is portable to other networks (the [V2 paper] shows gains on ResNet and V1 too).
5. For this toolkit: when implementing or benchmarking these models, prefer dense fused convs early and depthwise convs late, and expect measured throughput to diverge from FLOP counts.
